# Lifestyle cover refresh plan

## Scope and finding

The publication receipts in `.tmp/live-batch/publication/receipt.json` and `.tmp/publication-harness/runs/*/publication/receipt.json` record six product themes, four articles each, and two destinations per article: 24 Shopify Journal pages and 24 Substack posts. The 24 saved covers have distinct SHA-256 hashes. The visual problem is repeated isolated-object composition, not a reused image file. This plan covers all 24 article pairs and future four-article batches. The receipts are historical evidence; verify every live page again before updating it.

The original `extract` or `extract-from-craft` → `score` → `article` → `translate` → `distribute` creation sequence remains unchanged. This work changes cover preparation and later published-cover replacement only. Keep existing article IDs, handles, URLs, bodies, and product links unless a factual review finds a separate error. Substack delivery stays web-only; editing a published post must not trigger email or app delivery.

## Visual contract

1. Read the current Shopify product page and obtain its original photographs, including the exact variant planned for the cover. Record dimensions, silhouette, rim, foot, material, color, pattern, and any visible packaging. Use photographs as **image inputs** to the generation tool. A URL in a text prompt is insufficient.
2. Build one scene brief for each article angle. Show a believable everyday moment: breakfast, afternoon tea, quiet dinner, hosting, a gift being opened, or a table being cleared. The photographed craft must remain the visual anchor, but it belongs in a lived-in environment with restrained props. Each of four covers must differ in activity, camera position, lighting, and prop arrangement.
3. Preserve product proportions and details from the source photographs and measurements. Use known-scale props only when they help check size. Do not invent a maker mark, decorative motif, variant, care method, price, or food-contact claim. If the required source angle or variant cannot be verified, choose another scene or stop that cover.
   When a generated object differs from the listing, use the real product cutout as the visual anchor in a generated room or table scene and match its shadows and perspective. Reject the image if that composite still looks implausible.
4. Review at full resolution and in a three-column Journal contact sheet. Check physical plausibility, visual variety within the theme and across neighboring themes, title/scene fit, and clean crops on desktop and mobile. Keep each exported cover at least 1536×1024. Save the prompt, source URLs, image hashes, dimensions, chosen variant, review note, and rejected candidates outside Git.
5. Treat generated scenes as editorial illustrations of use. Keep the real product photograph available on the linked product page. Never claim the synthetic cover is a literal photograph of the listed item.

## Replacement sequence and recovery

1. Build a 24-row refresh manifest from the six receipts. Include the exact Shopify article ID, both verified URLs, old cover hash and URL, product URL, variant, dimensions, source image URLs, scene brief, and destination states. Do not mutate the original completed receipts or mark a refresh complete using their historic `verifiedAt` times.
2. Refresh one four-article theme first. Generate and review all four candidates together. Reject misleading object details and compositions that read alike in the Journal grid. Keep the old images and destination URLs for rollback. Continue by theme only after the first set passes visual review.
3. Use Shopify `stagedUploadsCreate`, upload each approved cover to its returned target, then pass the `resourceUrl` to `articleUpdate.image.url` for the exact existing Journal article ID. This route was verified on a public article and does not require `fileCreate` or `write_files`. Preserve title, body, handle, publication status, and product link. Verify the public article and Journal card show the new image; record the observed image URL and time.
4. Open the matching published Substack post in the authenticated editor. Replace its first image with the approved matching cover without creating a new post or initiating delivery. Verify the public post's first image and Shopify link. Record the observed image URL and time. If the editor offers an email/app resend option, leave it off; if the behavior is unclear, stop.
5. Mark an article pair complete only after both public pages show the correct new cover. Keep separate `prepared`, `shopify-updated`, `shopify-verified`, `substack-updated`, and `substack-verified` states with old/new image hashes and URLs. On uncertainty, read back the live page before retrying. If a replacement fails, use the saved old image URL to restore the affected page and verify it.
6. After all 24 pairs, audit the Journal grid, article pages, and Substack first images again. Confirm 48 live page checks and 24 matching image pairs. Do not claim completion from successful uploads alone.

The current publication ledger handles creation, not edits to already completed posts. Implement a separate refresh ledger and bounded update commands before any live replacement. Do not reuse `publication-harness.mjs cover`, which is intentionally immutable after publication.

## Journal order and future batches

The live Journal cards follow publication time. Keep the historical dates of these 24 posts. Their visual order is improved by varying scene, viewpoint, light, and verified finish within each four-article theme; do not change dates merely to rearrange cards. When two future four-article themes are ready together, alternate theme A and theme B publication events when practical while preserving every article's evidence and web-only Substack setting. Confirm the final Journal card order after publishing.

For a new product batch, `prepare-publication.mjs` requires `--visual-evidence`. Its JSON must include the current product URL, actual local product image files, verified dimensions and features, and four distinct scenes. Send the actual files as image inputs to the image generation tool. Review all four candidates as a contact sheet and reject a candidate if its object differs materially from the product photograph.
